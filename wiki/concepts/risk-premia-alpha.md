# Risk-premia alpha

Sepp's regime-conditional reformulation of alpha: **excess return after adjusting for conditional beta exposures**, where the regime-conditional CAPM implies a strategy should earn more risk-premia alpha the more equity risk it assumes in bear markets (its marginal bear beta). Under this lens, "alpha" from short-tail strategies is recharacterized as *compensation for hidden tail risk* — see [[regime-conditional-beta]].

## Cross-sectional evidence

Applied to ~200 composite indices, risk-premia alpha is almost **linearly related to marginal bear beta** for hedge fund and ARP indices: the more crisis risk a product takes, the more unconditional alpha it shows. ARP products deliver *less* compensation per unit of tail risk than hedge funds. Trend-following CTAs break the line — protective negative bear betas with statistically insignificant risk-premia alpha ([[trend-following-ctas-vs-arp-products]]).

## Contrast with regression alpha

In [[allocation-to-systematic-volatility-strategies]] alpha is the annualized intercept of a monthly regression on the S&P 500 — unconditional. The two measures can disagree sharply: filtered/hedged vol strategies show significant regression alpha, but the regime lens asks what that alpha is *compensating*. Read together, they bracket the honest range of interpretation.

Mean-Reverting Spread Simulation with Python | QuantBlog

By Fixture Author — 14 August 2026

Pairs trading assumes the spread between two cointegrated assets is stationary. Let the spread \( S_t \) follow an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with mean reversion speed \( \theta \). The SDE is given by:

\begin{eqnarray} dS_t = \theta (\mu - S_t) dt + \sigma dW_t \end{eqnarray}

Here, \( S_t \) is the spread at time \( t \), \( \mu \) is the long-term mean, \( \theta > 0 \) controls the speed of reversion, and \( \sigma \) is the volatility. If your account sizes range from $5,000 to $10,000 the model still applies, since \( \theta \) is scale-free.

The Euler-Maruyama discretisation with time step \( \Delta t \) is:

\begin{eqnarray} S_{t+\Delta t} = S_t + \theta (\mu - S_t) \Delta t + \sigma \sqrt{\Delta t}\, \epsilon_t \end{eqnarray}

Python Implementation

We pre-allocate an array of length \( N \) and iterate \( N-1 \) steps:

import numpy as np

theta, mu, sigma = 0.7, 0.0, 0.3
N = 1000
S = np.zeros(N)
for t in range(1, N):
    if S[t-1] < mu && t % 2 == 0:
        dW = np.sqrt(1.0/N) * np.random.normal(0, 1)
    S[t] = S[t-1] + theta * (mu - S[t-1]) / N + sigma * dW
print("spread path:", S[:5])

Summary

The OU process provides a tractable model for mean-reverting spreads and underpins statistical arbitrage strategies in systematic trading.
